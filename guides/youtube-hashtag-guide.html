How to Use YouTube Hashtags Thoughtfully

Choose relevant YouTube hashtags that describe your video without stuffing descriptions or chasing unrelated trends.

Start with the actual subject

Choose hashtags that accurately describe the content, such as its topic, format, or intended audience. If a viewer follows the hashtag, the video should make sense among other posts on that subject.

Prefer relevance over popularity

A popular hashtag is not automatically a good choice. Avoid unrelated trending tags, celebrity names, or broad topics that do not appear in your video. Misleading labels can frustrate viewers.

Use a small, focused set

A few precise hashtags are easier to understand than a long block. Select the main subject first, then add a narrower technique, format, or audience tag only if it adds useful context.

Check spelling and unintended meanings

Review each tag for typos, confusing abbreviations, and meanings in other contexts. Search the hashtag before using it to make sure the surrounding content is relevant and appropriate.

Do not confuse hashtags with tags

Hashtags are visible labels written with a # symbol in titles or descriptions. Video tags are a separate metadata field. They serve different purposes, so do not paste a long hashtag list into every field.

Measure the whole viewer experience

Hashtags are only one small part of publishing. Review whether the title and thumbnail set accurate expectations, whether the opening delivers on the promise, and whether viewers find the video useful.

Example

Example: For a tutorial teaching beginner guitar chords, relevant hashtags might describe beginner guitar lessons, guitar chords, and the particular technique shown. Avoid tags for genres or artists that are not part of the video.

Keep in mind: Platform rules can change, including how hashtags are displayed or limited. Review current YouTube Help guidance and never use misleading or unrelated hashtags.
